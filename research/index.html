Research

My research is in macroeconomics and international macroeconomics. I study how household heterogeneity and international linkages shape the transmission of fiscal policy and the optimal design of monetary and fiscal policy.

Job Market Paper

Revising the Separation: Optimal Monetary-Fiscal Mix in Open Economy

Daolin Bai, Kang Shi (Tsinghua U), Juanyi Xu (HKUST), and Vivian Zhanwei Yue (Emory U)

October 2026 · Draft coming soon

Abstract

This paper studies the optimal cooperative monetary–fiscal policy mix in a two-country New Keynesian open economy with heterogeneous households and transfer-based redistribution. We show that, given imperfect international risk sharing, consumption inequality affects external adjustment, the terms of trade, and inflation dynamics. As a result, the separation principle breaks down: optimal stabilization of inflation and output cannot be decoupled from the cross-country allocation of consumption inequality. In the cooperative optimum, monetary and fiscal policy jointly implement a non-zero exposure of relative consumption inequality to shocks, implying that full insurance is generally not optimal even when transfer instruments are available. We further show that this interaction remains central under Local Currency Pricing (LCP), where deviations from the law of one price strengthen the welfare relevance of coordinated monetary and fiscal responses.

Fiscal Multipliers in Open Economies with Heterogeneous Households

Daolin Bai, Kang Shi (Tsinghua U), Juanyi Xu (HKUST), and Vivian Zhanwei Yue (Emory U)

October 2026 · Draft coming soon

Abstract

Household heterogeneity can either amplify or dampen fiscal multipliers in open economies, depending on the monetary regime. This paper develops a tractable two-country, two-agent New Keynesian model that yields closed-form solutions for fiscal multipliers and international spillovers. Under strict inflation stabilization, a larger hand-to-mouth share strengthens terms-of-trade appreciation, lowers the domestic multiplier, and raises international spillovers. With constant real interest rates, this relative-price channel weakens and heterogeneity amplifies both effects. We derive an analytical threshold for the monetary responsiveness in a Taylor rule that governs this reversal. Full stabilization under cooperative monetary policy requires Keynesian tax incidence aligning with the composition of government spending. The mechanism continues to operate under local currency pricing and alternative calibration. Cross-country evidence supports significantly lower multipliers and short-run terms-of-trade appreciation in economies with larger hand-to-mouth shares under flexible regimes.
